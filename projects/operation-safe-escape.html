Designing a secure case-management platform for Operation Safe Escape

Operation Safe Escape supports survivors of domestic violence, stalking, and harassment. Through Hack4Impact, I designed parts of FLOW, a secure platform meant to bring OSE’s case work out of email threads and spreadsheets and into one place.

Role
Product Designer
Team
Hack4Impact, for Operation Safe Escape
Timeline
Sept–Dec 2025
Focus
  • Product design

Problem
OSE tracked hundreds of survivor cases through email and spreadsheets, so admins and volunteers had to piece a case together before they could act on it.
My contribution
I designed five FLOW workflows, from survivor intake to the case dashboard, and contributed to the shared component system.

Context

Every case lived across email threads and spreadsheets

OSE’s case history was spread across emails and spreadsheets, so a volunteer picking up an assignment depended on other people’s notes to understand what had already happened. FLOW gave us a way to design around that handoff, with the case history and the next steps available to the people working on it.

Robert Gomez

Composite persona

OSE volunteer who sees only his assigned cases and depends on other people’s notes to pick up where they left off.

What he needs
To open a case and see how urgent it is, what the survivor needs, and what has already happened, then leave a clear update for whoever works on it next.

Constraints

Safety came before convenience

Access follows the assignment

Volunteers needed enough context to pick up their assigned cases, while the information they could see had to stay within the scope of that assignment. That made access part of the workflow itself, from what appeared in the case list to what someone could open and update.

An ongoing process

Cases move between survivors, admins, and volunteers over time, so the workflow had to keep enough history for someone new to understand what already happened.

A lot of information per case

Each case held a lot of detail, so I put the most important information first and kept the rest available when someone needed it.

Building shared components

OSE didn’t have a product design system, so our team built the components FLOW needed around its existing logo and typography, which kept flows designed in parallel consistent.

Intake

Designing intake for survivors and for the team that maintains it

01

Designing intake for a phone

Survivors needed to be able to complete intake without a desktop, so I designed it mobile-first. A Safety Exit stays in the same place on every screen, the intro explains how long the form takes and that questions can be skipped, and a progress bar shows how much is left.

02

A form builder OSE can maintain

OSE doesn’t have developers on staff, so the team needed to update intake questions after handoff without relying on us. The form builder offers several question types, and admins can preview a form before publishing it to survivors.

Case workspace

One place to understand a case, update it, and pass it along

03

Helping admins see which cases need attention

Admins see every case in one list, while volunteers see only the cases assigned to them. Sortable columns bring the cases that need attention to the top, and priority and case-type labels make urgency easier to read before opening anything.

We designed the CSV export to help OSE prepare the documentation it had been assembling by hand for law enforcement.

04

Keeping a case’s story in one view

Opening a case brings its details together, so an admin or volunteer isn’t piecing the story together from separate records. Priority, status, and case type stay on the left, and we designed Send Account Creation Email to take over a message admins had been writing by hand after escalating a case.

05

Moving conversations off personal email

Secure messaging gives survivors, volunteers, and admins one place to talk instead of personal email threads, with role labels and color cues that show who is speaking.

How it connects

From intake to a shared case record

Intake starts the record, admins review and update it, volunteers pick up assigned work, and messaging keeps the conversation attached to the same case. After handoff, the form builder keeps the intake questions up to date.

Outcome

What we handed off

I handed off designs for mobile intake, the form builder, the case dashboard, the case card, and messaging, along with the shared components we used across them. Together, those pieces show how a case could move from a survivor’s first submission to the staff and volunteers responsible for following up.

What I took from the project

On FLOW, I kept coming back to what a volunteer would need to know when they picked up a case someone else had been working on. They needed enough history to understand what had already happened, within the access they had to that case. Thinking through that handoff changed how I approached the case workspace, because the notes and conversations one person left behind would become the starting point for the next.
