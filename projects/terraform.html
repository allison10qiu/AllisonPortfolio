Making read-only access clearer in Terraform

During my Product Design internship at IBM, I worked on Terraform's role-based access control experience. My project focused on how people could review configuration information without automatically having permission to change it.

01 Internship overview

Read-only turned out to be more than removing the edit button.

Different Terraform users need different levels of access depending on what they're trying to do. I worked on making those boundaries clearer, so someone reviewing a configuration could understand what they were seeing, what they could change, and what was intentionally unavailable.

41 Routes

I audited and designed around 41 settings and configuration routes to understand how a read-only pattern could work consistently across Terraform.

10+ Research participants

I worked on research across auditors, support engineers, and platform engineers to understand how different users review and investigate configurations.

3 Workflows

The work covered three different workflows: auditing configurations, troubleshooting issues, and reviewing settings before making changes.

02 Who we designed for

The same read-only experience had to support three different jobs.

Auditors

Need to review settings and policies without needing permission to change them.

Support engineers

Need enough configuration context to investigate why something isn't working, even when they aren't the person who can make the fix.

Platform engineers

Move between reviewing and changing configurations, so the difference between read and edit needs to stay clear.
