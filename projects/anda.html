Deciding what Anda’s team should build first

Anda helps drivers in Angola earn a living while working toward owning their vehicles. Through Hack4Impact, I was the product manager for an admin and Customer Success platform built on driver data that was scattered across four systems.

Problem
Driver activity and payment data lived in Odoo, spreadsheets, and GPS and Yango APIs, so no one could tell when a driver actually needed support.

Context

No one could tell when a driver needed support

An inactive driver could be underperforming, dealing with a vehicle issue, or falling behind on payments, and admins couldn’t tell which. Checking on one driver meant opening several disconnected systems, and there was no clear way to decide who needed help most urgently.

Afonso Ndalu

Customer Success Manager

Checks Odoo, GPS, Yango, and spreadsheets to decide which driver to check on first, and needs to understand why a driver was flagged before reaching out.

What he needs
A ranked view of who needs help most urgently and why, grounded in activity and payment signals he can explain to a driver.

The data

We had to know which data we could rely on first

Driver activity and payment data came from four places, and their reliability shaped what we could prioritize. The screens would only be useful if that data held up, so I worked with our tech lead on the data model before we scoped any views.

Odoo

The system of record for driver and payment records.

Spreadsheets

Filled in by hand, so gaps and errors were possible.

GPS API

Vehicle movement, speed, and engine status.

Yango API

Trip and earnings activity from the ride-hailing side.

Roadmap

From understanding driver performance to prioritizing outreach

P0 · Make driver performance visible

I scoped authentication, the Driver Performance Overview, and the admin and Customer Success dashboards for the first release. Together, they gave staff a place to review driver activity and payments and see which signals contributed to an individual’s performance score.

P1 · Help Customer Success prioritize outreach

I scoped the Priority Queue next, using driver activity and payment signals to help Customer Success Managers decide who to check on first.

Key decisions

Two calls that shaped the roadmap

01

Show what goes into a driver’s performance score

I made the score breakdown a P0 requirement because a single number gave staff little to investigate. Seeing the factors behind it gave them a place to start, whether that meant looking more closely at payment timeliness, hours worked, or another input.

02

Explain why a driver is flagged

A ranked queue could tell a Customer Success Manager where to start, but they still needed to know why a driver appeared near the top before reaching out. We used synthetic data to keep design and development moving while access to real driver data was delayed, which left the ranking itself to be validated after the semester.

Team output

What the team designed from those requirements

Tiffany Liu and Isabella Raffo designed the interfaces. Each screen traces back to a requirement in the roadmap.

  1. Vehicle activity

    From the P0 requirement that inactivity carry context: moving, parked, or offline for a longer stretch.

  2. Revenue and default rate

    The payment half of the P0 data, shown as trends rather than a single balance.

  3. Manage users

    Verification and roles, with Drivers Assigned so admins can see each CSM’s workload.

  4. Authentication

    Role-based access shipped with the core data, as a prerequisite rather than a feature.

Outcome

What we shipped to Anda

We shipped authentication, the Driver Performance Overview and score breakdown, the admin and Customer Success dashboards, and the Priority Queue. The driver app, rewards, and messaging stayed out of scope so the team could focus on giving staff a clearer picture of driver activity and payments. How well the queue would prioritize outreach with real driver data remained open at the end of the semester.

What I took from the project

Owning my first roadmap taught me how much a decision about what to build depends on understanding the data behind it. We shipped a queue to help staff decide who to check on first, though we still needed real driver data to see whether the ranking held up. Working through that with our tech lead helped me get more comfortable asking technical questions until I understood what the team could move forward with and what we still needed to learn.
