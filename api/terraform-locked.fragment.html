Read-only still needed context.

ResearchMulti-segment · 10+ participants, internal and external

We researched what people still needed from configurations they couldn’t change. I helped prepare the questions, co-facilitated sessions, and synthesized the findings.

07CombinedExploration · not released during my internship

The answer wasn’t M or H.

I combined what worked from both.

Testing exposed a trade-off: M made the saved configuration easier to find, while H preserved context for unfamiliar settings. I used those findings to explore a combined direction that pulled from both. This direction remained exploratory and was not released during my internship.

08Up close

How the principles translated into specific controls.

Decision 01Principle 1

Selected values carry the weight. Alternatives stay quiet.

0102
01Current value carries the weight02Alternatives remain as context
Evidence
Showing only the saved value made some settings harder to interpret. Showing every option at equal weight made the current configuration harder to scan.
Design response
The selected value became the strongest visual anchor. Unselected options stayed visible when they helped explain the range of possible values, but moved into the background.
What I gave up
A shorter values-only summary. The read-only page stays closer to the length of the editable one.
Decision 02Principle 3

Dependent settings stay attached to what controls them.

0102
01Controlling setting02Settings that depend on it, indented beneath
Evidence
Some values only make sense because of a setting above them. Without that link, the read-only page was accurate but hard to follow.
Design response
I kept dependent settings grouped under the setting that drives them, so a reviewer can see why a value exists even without edit access.
Decision 03Principle 2

Descriptions stay when they answer a question.

I didn’t want every description to survive just because it was on the editable page. I kept one when the setting was unfamiliar or the value couldn’t explain itself.

KEEPThe description explains something the value alone cannot. REMOVEThe value already communicates enough on its own.

13Reflection

This project changed how I think about designing for complex products.

01
Designing less isn’t always simplifying.

I started by assuming a cleaner read-only experience meant showing less. Research showed me that simplification has to preserve the information people need to make sense of a system, not just reduce what appears on the screen.

02
Think in rules, not pages

Working across 41 routes pushed me to stop treating each screen as a separate design problem and start defining behaviors that could hold up across the product.
